Road Damage Augmentation Experiments and Results
Detailed project report through 27 October 2026
This report documents the implementation, completed experiments, verified results, and unresolved
methodological issues in the road-damage augmentation project. It covers the early crop-classification study,
detector feasibility and debugging, the five-condition Faster R-CNN comparison, and the subsequent diagnosis
of the proposed online class-adaptive controller. Proposed changes are separated from completed work.
The main finding is negative for the proposed accuracy claim. The class-adaptive controller runs and its
decisions can be audited, but its selected validation AP50:95 of 22.563 is below online shared local
augmentation at 23.059 and identity at 22.823. Each condition has only one training run. These results establish
a functioning prototype and a documented comparison, not a reliable generalization advantage.
1 Research objective and current conclusion
The research question is whether local appearance augmentation can be selected online according to the damage
classes present in an overlapping group of annotations, while preserving the detector’s ability to recognize those
objects. A common policy may be limited by the most sensitive class. Conditioning decisions on the actual class
combination could allow different strengths in different regions. This is the motivating hypothesis; it is not an
established property of the completed system.
The proposed contribution is the augmentation controller: overlap-group construction, a shared local edit,
detector-based retention constraints, class-balanced difficulty feedback, exact-class-set policy selection, and
reproducible online updates. The full-image experiments use an existing Faster R-CNN with a ResNet-50
feature pyramid backbone and COCO initialization. The detector architecture itself is not newly designed. The
earlier crop classifier was a separate, custom network trained from scratch.
Five detection conditions have completed the same 20-epoch optimizer budget: identity, fixed image-level
augmentation, fixed local augmentation, online shared local augmentation, and online class-adaptive local
augmentation. Online shared has the highest observed selected AP50:95; identity has the highest observed
selected AP50. Class adaptation is nearly equal numerically to fixed local in this run and lower than its primary
online-shared comparator. No statistical equivalence, significant difference, or population-level ranking can be
inferred from one run per condition. [S3, S4]
The next justified step is a focused diagnostic of the feedback measurement and probe support, followed by
short controlled comparisons if the diagnostic succeeds. Another unchanged full run would estimate variability
but would not resolve the identified design weaknesses. No revised controller, repeated-seed study, or final test
evaluation is included in the present results.

3 3 Shared preprocessing and adapters
Full images are converted to RGB, resized with aspect ratio preserved, and letterboxed to 640 by 640 pixels.
Box scaling uses the actual resized dimensions, including separate axis scales caused by integer size rounding.
Detector adapters map the foreground labels consistently; Faster R-CNN reserves label zero for background and
uses labels one through four for damage. Target-negative images remain valid training examples. Evaluation
transforms predictions back to the original stored coordinates and clips them to the image bounds.
4 Methodology of the proposed solution
4 1 Local groups and a single shared edit
For each prepared target-bearing image, target boxes are rasterized by flooring minima and ceiling maxima
within the valid image content. An overlap graph connects boxes whose raster supports share positive area. Each
connected component forms one augmentation group, including singleton boxes. The group class set contains
every distinct damage class represented in that component. Four classes allow 15 possible nonempty exact sets.
[S2]
At an augmentation opportunity, one group is selected uniformly. A candidate appearance transform is
computed from the full resized image content and composited once within the union of the selected group’s
boxes. This avoids repeatedly transforming pixels where annotations overlap. The union is a box-based support,
not a pixel-level damage segmentation; background inside a box can also change.
x̃ = x + αG ⊙ [To,m(x) − x] (1)
In Equation 1, x is the prepared image, T is operation o at strength m, and α is zero outside the group union, one
in its one-pixel-eroded interior, and 0.5 on the outer eight-neighbor boundary layer. A one-pixel-wide support
retains alpha 0.5. Candidate operations and final composition use the declared clipping and uint8 rounding rules.
The labels and coordinates remain fixed. Preserving coordinates does not prove that every edit preserves
visually recognizable damage.
4 2 Candidate augmentation bank
The first controller uses a bounded appearance-only bank to keep annotations geometrically valid and make
feedback interpretable. The photometric family contains brightness up/down, contrast up/down, and gamma
brightening/darkening. The second family contains Gaussian blur and Gaussian noise. A larger bank, including
geometric transforms, would require additional label transformation and validity checks and has not been tested
here. Restricting this first study is an implementation control, not a claim that other augmentations are
ineffective.

Noise is independent across pixels and RGB channels in the stored RGB/255 representation. Contrast uses the
full-content mean luminance. These operations act on stored color values rather than linear-light intensities.
Fixed controls use level 2. They sample the two families with equal probability and sample operations uniformly
within the selected family. Therefore, each photometric operation has conditional probability 1/12 and each
blur/noise operation has probability 1/4; the eight operations are not globally equiprobable.
4 3 Paired online probes
The controller updates before epochs 2, 4, 6, through 20, after one unaugmented warmup epoch. Policies remain
fixed between updates. Each update selects 256 target-bearing training images in total using keyed hash ranking,
preferring distinct source clusters, and then selects one group per image uniformly. This is not a budget of 256
images per class or per class set. The shared and class-adaptive online conditions use the same prospective
image/group panels.
Every selected image supplies an unmodified reference view and transformed views at three strengths for all
eight operations. Gaussian noise uses two independent draws, each paired across strengths. The other seven
operations use one view per strength. This gives 28 image forwards per probe image: one reference plus 27
transformed views. Ten updates require 71,680 additional image forwards per online run, plus auxiliary ROI
computations.
Probes use training data in evaluation/inference mode and do not take optimizer steps. The implementation
preserves parameters, buffers, gradients, and random-number-generator state across probing. Policy decisions
and noise have separate keyed random streams. Policies are stored with training checkpoints; resuming a
committed update does not run it again. A probe interrupted before commitment is reconstructed from the same
checkpoint, with partial work recorded separately.
4 4 Retention and exact class set support
The controller first measures whether damage that the current detector recognizes remains detectable after
augmentation. Standard predictions are matched to ground-truth boxes one-to-one, in descending score order,
requiring the correct class, confidence at least 0.25, and IoU at least 0.5. Matching is performed against the full
image; retention then uses objects in the selected group that were detected in the reference view.

Here s is the exact group class set, c is a class in s, and J(c,s) contains class-c objects in selected groups with

object before aggregation, so they do not count as independent support. A class that is poorly detected initially

stability of existing detections; it cannot certify initially missed objects or semantic correctness.
Every represented class in an exact set must have at least eight distinct reference-detected group objects
spanning at least four source clusters. Each family-strength candidate must then retain at least 80% for every
represented class and every operation in that family. This is a joint constraint: one sensitive class or operation
can reject the family-strength candidate. The minimum support reduces decisions based on one or two detections,

4 6 Training loop and comparator distinction
After warmup, each target-bearing training image has an augmentation opportunity with probability 0.5. A
selected group receives its current exact-set policy, or identity if the policy cannot act. Images without targets
remain unchanged. Keyed decisions use the run seed, epoch, image identifier, and a purpose-specific key
independently of global training randomness. Training then uses the normal Faster R-CNN detection loss and
optimizer update; the controller does not introduce a separate learned network or backpropagate through its
decisions.
Ldet = LRPN cls + LRPN box + LROI cls + LROI box (8)
The online shared local control uses the same operations, panels, grid, retention threshold, difficulty
construction, and update schedule, but pools selected groups across exact class sets into one shared policy with
constraints over all four classes. The proposed condition restricts feedback and routing to each exact class set.
This comparison changes support and downstream edit exposure as a consequence of conditioning. It does not
isolate class routing while holding every realized transformation fixed.

5 1 CCAST execution and reproducibility
Full experiments ran as PBS batch jobs on CCAST using one NVIDIA A10, four allocated CPU cores, 32 GB
requested RAM, and a 24-hour wall-time request. The batch workflow checks package integrity, activates the
project environment, runs its validation stages, records training output, and writes recoverable checkpoints.
Submission occurs from a login node; the scheduler supplies the GPU allocation. A disconnected SSH
monitoring session does not by itself terminate an already submitted batch job.
The recorded full-run software is Python 3.12.3, PyTorch 2.3.0, TorchVision 0.18.0, CUDA build 12.1, NumPy
1.26.4, Pillow 10.3.0, and pycocotools 2.0.10. YOLO development additionally used ultralytics-opencv-headless
8.4.182 and opencv-python-headless 4.11.0.86. Local verification used its separately recorded local versions;
those results are not described as target-environment executions.
Code packages, manifests, weights, configurations, and returned archives are pinned by SHA-256. Full
checkpoint binaries remain on CCAST, while the downloaded records contain configuration, metrics,
predictions, controller observations, decisions, and checkpoint metadata. Saved predictions support independent
metric recomputation without rerunning detector inference. Seed matching and deterministic augmentation
decisions do not imply bitwise-identical GPU training: full deterministic algorithms were not enabled, and the
measured warmup trajectories differ.

7 Observed controller behavior
7 1 Online shared policy
The shared controller acted on all 158,151 scheduled augmentation opportunities, and 156,819 applications
changed pixels. Its class support was sufficient at all ten updates. It selected blur/noise level 1 at every update;
photometric strength varied across levels 1, 2, and 3 and was initially ineligible. When both families were
eligible, probabilities stayed near one half. [S5]
This behavior provides a concrete explanation for why the online condition is not equivalent to the fixed level-2
local baseline. Most blur/noise edits became milder, while photometric settings changed over training. The
observed AP difference cannot distinguish the value of the feedback mechanism from the value of this realized

8 Retrospective diagnosis of strength and support
After the completed class-adaptive run, saved training observations and frozen training metadata were examined
without new optimizer steps, detector inference, or test evaluation. The analysis reconstructs decisions on the
observed trajectory. It cannot predict the accuracy of a controller that would have changed that trajectory. [S12]
8 1 Strongest passing is not the same as most useful
Of 95 active family/update/class-set states, 52 had exactly zero clipped net difficulty. Those states accounted for
97,048 of 144,568 applied edits, or 67.13%. In 44 states the selected strength had zero difficulty although
another eligible strength had a positive score; those states accounted for 82,914 applications. This follows from
the implemented rule: strength maximizes the passing level, while difficulty is used only after strength selection.
For example, the D00 photometric state at epoch 4 selected level 3 with difficulty zero and minimum retention
about 0.907; all three levels were eligible and level 1 had a positive difficulty of about 0.0088. This is evidence

that the maximum-level rule can ignore the measured difficulty ordering. It is not evidence that level 1 would
produce better validation AP.
For 94.26% of applied edits, both family probabilities were within 0.05 of one half. Consequently, much of this
run’s adaptation was expressed through admissible strength and fallback behavior rather than sharply different
family weights. A zero clipped net score does not imply an unchanged image, no harder object, or no possible
regularization benefit. The score’s aggregation and clipping prevent those stronger interpretations.
A leave-one-source-cluster-out sensitivity check found six selected states whose retention decision failed under
at least one cluster deletion. They routed 3,186 applications. This is a descriptive sensitivity check, not a
confidence interval or a formal significance test; nevertheless, it shows that the minimum support threshold does
not guarantee a stable decision.
8 2 The auxiliary loss compares changing ROI samples
Across 20,752 correlated object-operation comparisons at selected states, the number of assigned positive ROIs
changed in 86.64% of cases. Auxiliary loss decreased in 77.90%. The mean ROI count rose from 15.05 in the
reference view to 16.87 in the transformed view. In zero-score states, mean count rose from 15.91 to 18.19 and
loss decreased in 86.49% of comparisons.
The measured association between loss change and ROI-count change was approximately -0.534. These
repeated observations are correlated, so the coefficient is descriptive and does not establish causation. More
fundamentally, the observer averages over a different proposal sample after the transformation. An apparent loss
decrease can combine an appearance effect with changes in which ROIs are measured. It is therefore too strong
to interpret this auxiliary difference as an isolated estimate of object difficulty.
The records do not support the specific explanation that harder proposals disappeared and caused the decrease;
average proposal counts increased. Saved feedback contains object summaries and ROI counts but not the full
per-ROI boxes and logits required to reconstruct a fixed-ROI counterfactual. A new frozen-checkpoint probe is
needed to evaluate that measurement change.
8 3 Sparse panels rather than absent training examples

Annotation availability alone implies that at least 8,798 of the total 13,583 fallbacks could not have been
avoided on the old panels even with perfect reference detection. Thus a meaningful part of the support problem
is panel allocation, not just detector failure.

9 Verification and evidential limits
The latest class-adaptive archive contains 123 members. Member checksums and checksum coverage were
verified, archived source bytes match the released package, and configuration/environment records match the
declared protocol. The frozen training/validation separation, original-coordinate validation ground truth, epoch
histories, prediction coverage and geometry, training budget, and best/final checkpoint metadata were checked.
[S4]
Independent COCO evaluation recomputed the selected epoch 18 and final epoch 20, including per-class metrics,
with maximum discrepancy zero. All ten controller updates and all 15 exact-set states per update were
reconstructed from saved per-object observations using an independent aggregation implementation. Training
decisions, sample order, group geometry, fallbacks, and exposure counts were replayed across all 20 epochs.
Earlier conditions retain their prior independent metric audits; the comparison also checked their original
archive hashes and saved records.
These audits do not constitute independent reruns of the detector or raw-pixel pipeline. Full checkpoint tensors
were not re-executed locally, and raw probe boxes/logits were not archived. Reported pixel-change counters and
state-preservation flags were checked for consistency with source and records, not reproduced from a fresh live
GPU model. Unit tests and successful training are complementary evidence, not substitutes for performance
confirmation.
The principal scientific limitations are one seed per condition, checkpoint selection on the same validation set
used for reporting, a narrow operation bank, no full YOLO or scratch-initialization replication, no matched
fixed-strength realization control, unequal online forward cost, and unequal edit exposure after class-set
fallbacks. Warmup trajectory differences limit causal attribution. The observed support and ROI issues identify
plausible intervention targets, but do not prove which mechanism caused the AP result.
No held-out test score, cross-country generalization claim, severity-specific result, robustness benchmark,
deployment assessment, or repeated-seed uncertainty estimate is reported. The small selected-subset checks,
crop-classification results, and full-image validation scores answer different questions and remain separate
throughout the report.
10 Changes recommended before another full experiment
The following changes are proposed, not implemented or validated by the completed experiments. Their purpose
is to test the diagnosed mechanisms with less computation before launching another full run. Preserve the
version-1 results and thresholds as the historical comparison; a revised controller must be recorded as a new
condition.
10 1 Stabilize the auxiliary observer
At a frozen detector checkpoint, obtain reference-view ROIs and their object assignments once, then reuse those
ROI coordinates and labels for the reference and transformed feature maps. Continue to use the detector’s
ordinary view-specific predictions for retention. This separates a fixed-sample appearance-loss comparison from
the ordinary detection-stability measurement. Archive the ROI identities, boxes, labels, logits, and losses needed
to reproduce the observer. The new observer may change scores; improved AP remains an untested hypothesis.

10 2 Allocate probes to exact sets deliberately
Keep the total 256-image probe budget and distinct-source-cluster preference, but allocate support explicitly
across the four singleton and four common mixed sets. Resolve images or clusters shared by multiple strata
deterministically and report the actual sample budget. Retain the minimum eight detected objects and four
source clusters, and keep identity fallback for unresolved cases. Do not lower the retention threshold or borrow
unsupported class information merely to increase activity. Assess whether coverage gains cost too much support
in previously well-measured sets.
10 3 Test the strength rule separately
Compare the weakest admissible tested strength with the existing strongest-admissible rule under the same
retention threshold of 0.8. The weakest-passing choice is a diagnostic control, not a declared optimal method.
Do not replace the maximum-level rule with maximum difficulty until the observer’s proposal-composition
issue has been examined. A fixed mild-strength control and a uniform-family control can then distinguish the
benefit of feedback from the realized transformation distribution.
10 4 Use paired training starts and explicit continuation gates
Create a common unaugmented warmup checkpoint for each seed and branch comparison arms from the same
model, optimizer, random states, and subsequent sample order. Check repeatability before attributing a small
difference to policy. Record GPU/library settings and any remaining nondeterminism. One common checkpoint
improves pairing, but repeated seeds are still needed to estimate reliability.
First complete the frozen-checkpoint observer and support diagnostic. Continue only if it produces reproducible
measurements, preserves ordinary predictions outside the intended changes, and demonstrates usable support for
the prioritized sets at the fixed budget. Next run short paired training comparisons that change observer, panel
allocation, and strength rule separately or in an explicitly designed factorial study. Predeclare the accuracy,
support, exposure, and compute criteria before those outcomes are seen. No numerical AP improvement
threshold is retroactively invented in this report.
If those checks fail, further full-budget training should wait for the identified issue to be resolved. If they
succeed, freeze the revised protocol, execute matched seed repetitions, and reserve the test partition for the final
settled comparison. The project can currently claim a working and audited class-adaptive prototype with
documented negative development evidence. It cannot yet claim that class adaptation improves road-damage
detection.
