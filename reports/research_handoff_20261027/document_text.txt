1 Research objective and version 1 conclusion
The research question is whether local appearance augmentation can be selected online according to the damage classes present in an overlapping group of annotations, while preserving the detector’s ability to recognize those objects. A common policy may be limited by the most sensitive class. Conditioning decisions on the actual class combination could allow different strengths in different regions. This is the motivating hypothesis; it is not an established property of the completed system.
The proposed contribution is the augmentation controller: overlap-group construction, a shared local edit, detector-based retention constraints, class-balanced difficulty feedback, exact-class-set policy selection, and reproducible online updates. The full-image experiments use an existing Faster R-CNN with a ResNet-50 feature pyramid backbone and COCO initialization. The detector architecture itself is not newly designed. The earlier crop classifier was a separate, custom network trained from scratch.
Five detection conditions have completed the same 20-epoch optimizer budget: identity, fixed image-level augmentation, fixed local augmentation, online shared local augmentation, and online class-adaptive local augmentation. Online shared has the highest observed selected AP50:95; identity has the highest observed selected AP50. Class adaptation is nearly equal numerically to fixed local in this run and lower than its primary online-shared comparator. No statistical equivalence, significant difference, or population-level ranking can be inferred from one run per condition. [S3, S4]
The version-1 results motivate revising the observation rule, support allocation and action bank. The subsequent redesign review and the latest version-2 discussion are documented in Sections 10 and 11. Version 2 has not been implemented or evaluated; the older version-1 recommendation is not the current execution specification.
2 Development history and experiment scope
2 1 Earlier crop classification study
The project initially used annotated road-damage crops as classification inputs. The completed conventional-augmentation baseline used a randomly initialized CNN with 695,732 trainable parameters, unweighted cross-entropy, AdamW, batch size 64, and a 20-epoch cosine learning-rate schedule. It selected epoch 20 by validation macro-F1. On 8,242 validation crops, 7,461 predictions were correct: accuracy was 90.524% and macro-F1 was 0.891457. [S6]

The largest directional error was D20 classified as D00: 223 crops, or 14.01% of D20 examples. A box-aspect-ratio comparator achieved 67.03% accuracy and 0.4439 macro-F1, while the majority-class comparator achieved 47.29% accuracy and 0.1605 macro-F1. The CNN exceeded those controls, but their results also showed why annotation geometry and source cues needed attention. The error pattern alone did not identify its cause.
The full-image formulation was adopted to study augmentation with localization, multiple objects, overlapping boxes, and images without target annotations. Crop accuracy is therefore historical feasibility evidence, not a baseline in the detection AP tables. The detection split was rebuilt with historical exposure and full-pool similarity groups taken into account; the crop and detection validation sets are not interchangeable.

3 3 Shared preprocessing and adapters
Full images are converted to RGB, resized with aspect ratio preserved, and letterboxed to 640 by 640 pixels. Box scaling uses the actual resized dimensions, including separate axis scales caused by integer size rounding. Detector adapters map the foreground labels consistently; Faster R-CNN reserves label zero for background and uses labels one through four for damage. Target-negative images remain valid training examples. Evaluation transforms predictions back to the original stored coordinates and clips them to the image bounds.
4 Implemented version 1 methodology
4 1 Local groups and a single shared edit
For each prepared target-bearing image, target boxes are rasterized by flooring minima and ceiling maxima within the valid image content. An overlap graph connects boxes whose raster supports share positive area. Each connected component forms one augmentation group, including singleton boxes. The group class set contains every distinct damage class represented in that component. Four classes allow 15 possible nonempty exact sets. [S2]
At an augmentation opportunity, one group is selected uniformly. A candidate appearance transform is computed from the full resized image content and composited once within the union of the selected group’s boxes. This avoids repeatedly transforming pixels where annotations overlap. The union is a box-based support, not a pixel-level damage segmentation; background inside a box can also change.
     (1)
In Equation 1, x is the prepared image, T is operation o at strength m, and α is zero outside the group union, one in its one-pixel-eroded interior, and 0.5 on the outer eight-neighbor boundary layer. A one-pixel-wide support retains alpha 0.5. Candidate operations and final composition use the declared clipping and uint8 rounding rules. The labels and coordinates remain fixed. Preserving coordinates does not prove that every edit preserves visually recognizable damage.
4 2 Candidate augmentation bank
The first controller uses a bounded appearance-only bank to keep annotations geometrically valid and make feedback interpretable. The photometric family contains brightness up/down, contrast up/down, and gamma brightening/darkening. The second family contains Gaussian blur and Gaussian noise. A larger bank, including geometric transforms, would require additional label transformation and validity checks and has not been tested here. Restricting this first study is an implementation control, not a claim that other augmentations are ineffective.
Table 7  Candidate strength grid

Noise is independent across pixels and RGB channels in the stored RGB/255 representation. Contrast uses the full-content mean luminance. These operations act on stored color values rather than linear-light intensities. Fixed controls use level 2. They sample the two families with equal probability and sample operations uniformly within the selected family. Therefore, each photometric operation has conditional probability 1/12 and each blur/noise operation has probability 1/4; the eight operations are not globally equiprobable.
4 3 Paired online probes
The controller updates before epochs 2, 4, 6, through 20, after one unaugmented warmup epoch. Policies remain fixed between updates. Each update selects 256 target-bearing training images in total using keyed hash ranking, preferring distinct source clusters, and then selects one group per image uniformly. This is not a budget of 256 images per class or per class set. The shared and class-adaptive online conditions use the same prospective image/group panels.
Every selected image supplies an unmodified reference view and transformed views at three strengths for all eight operations. Gaussian noise uses two independent draws, each paired across strengths. The other seven operations use one view per strength. This gives 28 image forwards per probe image: one reference plus 27 transformed views. Ten updates require 71,680 additional image forwards per online run, plus auxiliary ROI computations.
Probes use training data in evaluation/inference mode and do not take optimizer steps. The implementation preserves parameters, buffers, gradients, and random-number-generator state across probing. Policy decisions and noise have separate keyed random streams. Policies are stored with training checkpoints; resuming a committed update does not run it again. A probe interrupted before commitment is reconstructed from the same checkpoint, with partial work recorded separately.
4 4 Retention and exact class set support
The controller first measures whether damage that the current detector recognizes remains detectable after augmentation. Standard predictions are matched to ground-truth boxes one-to-one, in descending score order, requiring the correct class, confidence at least 0.25, and IoU at least 0.5. Matching is performed against the full image; retention then uses objects in the selected group that were detected in the reference view.
     (2)
Here s is the exact group class set, c is a class in s, and J(c,s) contains class-c objects in selected groups with exact set s. Each d indicates a class-correct matched detection. Repeated noise draws are averaged within the object before aggregation, so they do not count as independent support. A class that is poorly detected initially can have few eligible denominator objects even when many annotations exist. Retention measures conditional stability of existing detections; it cannot certify initially missed objects or semantic correctness.
Every represented class in an exact set must have at least eight distinct reference-detected group objects spanning at least four source clusters. Each family-strength candidate must then retain at least 80% for every represented class and every operation in that family. This is a joint constraint: one sensitive class or operation can reject the family-strength candidate. The minimum support reduces decisions based on one or two detections, but it is not a statistical precision guarantee.
     (3)
Equation 3 describes the implemented strength rule after support is sufficient. It selects the strongest passing tested level even if a weaker level fails; no monotonicity assumption is imposed. Missing or insufficiently supported sets and sets without an admissible family receive identity. Version 1 does not borrow support from another set, back off to the shared policy, retain a stale policy, or resample a different group to force an edit.
4 5 Auxiliary difficulty and family probabilities
For difficulty feedback, the detector’s evaluation RPN proposals are assigned to target objects by the ROI matcher. One explicitly self-assigned ground-truth ROI is also included per object so that a missing proposal does not remove that object’s auxiliary observation. These extra ground-truth ROIs are used only for feedback; they are not included as ordinary output detections.
     (4)
P is the assigned ROI set and n is its size for object j. The per-ROI term combines classification cross-entropy and class-specific SmoothL1 box loss, summed across four coordinates with beta 1/9. The controller excludes background and RPN loss from this difficulty measure. Loss is averaged over ROIs within an object, over repeated draws within an object, and then over all selected-group objects within each class and exact set, including reference misses. Only retention and support restrict to reference-detected objects. Reference and transformed views generate their own proposals in version 1; this later becomes an important measurement limitation.
     (5)
The mean is taken before subtraction and clipping in Equation 5. A harder object and an easier object may offset each other. A zero score therefore means that the normalized net class mean increase did not exceed zero, not that pixels were unchanged or every object became easier. The denominator offset 0.1 limits instability near zero reference loss, and the upper cap is 5.

     (7)
F is the set of eligible families. Equation 7 uses temperature 0.25 and exploration mass 0.2; with one eligible family its probability is one. An operation is then chosen uniformly within the selected family at its selected strength. Crucially, the difficulty score chooses family probabilities, not the strength within a family. Strength is already fixed by the maximum passing level in Equation 3. All numerical settings were engineering choices for this development protocol, not demonstrated optimal values.
4 6 Training loop and comparator distinction
After warmup, each target-bearing training image has an augmentation opportunity with probability 0.5. A selected group receives its current exact-set policy, or identity if the policy cannot act. Images without targets remain unchanged. Keyed decisions use the run seed, epoch, image identifier, and a purpose-specific key independently of global training randomness. Training then uses the normal Faster R-CNN detection loss and optimizer update; the controller does not introduce a separate learned network or backpropagate through its decisions.
     (8)
The online shared local control uses the same operations, panels, grid, retention threshold, difficulty construction, and update schedule, but pools selected groups across exact class sets into one shared policy with constraints over all four classes. The proposed condition restricts feedback and routing to each exact class set. This comparison changes support and downstream edit exposure as a consequence of conditioning. It does not isolate class routing while holding every realized transformation fixed.

8 Retrospective diagnosis of strength and support
After the completed class-adaptive run, saved training observations and frozen training metadata were examined without new optimizer steps, detector inference, or test evaluation. The analysis reconstructs decisions on the observed trajectory. It cannot predict the accuracy of a controller that would have changed that trajectory. [S12]
8 1 Strongest passing is not the same as most useful
Of 95 active family/update/class-set states, 52 had exactly zero clipped net difficulty. Those states accounted for 97,048 of 144,568 applied edits, or 67.13%. In 44 states the selected strength had zero difficulty although another eligible strength had a positive score; those states accounted for 82,914 applications. This follows from the implemented rule: strength maximizes the passing level, while difficulty is used only after strength selection.
For example, the D00 photometric state at epoch 4 selected level 3 with difficulty zero and minimum retention about 0.907; all three levels were eligible and level 1 had a positive difficulty of about 0.0088. This is evidence that the maximum-level rule can ignore the measured difficulty ordering. It is not evidence that level 1 would produce better validation AP.
For 94.26% of applied edits, both family probabilities were within 0.05 of one half. Consequently, much of this run’s adaptation was expressed through admissible strength and fallback behavior rather than sharply different family weights. A zero clipped net score does not imply an unchanged image, no harder object, or no possible regularization benefit. The score’s aggregation and clipping prevent those stronger interpretations.
A leave-one-source-cluster-out sensitivity check found six selected states whose retention decision failed under at least one cluster deletion. They routed 3,186 applications. This is a descriptive sensitivity check, not a confidence interval or a formal significance test; nevertheless, it shows that the minimum support threshold does not guarantee a stable decision.
8 2 The auxiliary loss compares changing ROI samples
Across 20,752 correlated object-operation comparisons at selected states, the number of assigned positive ROIs changed in 86.64% of cases. Auxiliary loss decreased in 77.90%. The mean ROI count rose from 15.05 in the reference view to 16.87 in the transformed view. In zero-score states, mean count rose from 15.91 to 18.19 and loss decreased in 86.49% of comparisons.
The measured association between loss change and ROI-count change was approximately -0.534. These repeated observations are correlated, so the coefficient is descriptive and does not establish causation. More fundamentally, the observer averages over a different proposal sample after the transformation. An apparent loss decrease can combine an appearance effect with changes in which ROIs are measured. It is therefore too strong to interpret this auxiliary difference as an isolated estimate of object difficulty.
The records do not support the specific explanation that harder proposals disappeared and caused the decrease; average proposal counts increased. Saved feedback contains object summaries and ROI counts but not the full per-ROI boxes and logits required to reconstruct a fixed-ROI counterfactual. A new frozen-checkpoint probe is needed to evaluate that measurement change.
8 3 Sparse panels rather than absent training examples
Among 20,709 opportunities for mixed-class groups, 12,119 received identity, a fallback fraction of 58.52%. Annotation availability alone implies that at least 8,798 of the total 13,583 fallbacks could not have been avoided on the old panels even with perfect reference detection. Thus a meaningful part of the support problem is panel allocation, not just detector failure.

9 Verification and evidential limits
The latest class-adaptive archive contains 123 members. Member checksums and checksum coverage were verified, archived source bytes match the released package, and configuration/environment records match the declared protocol. The frozen training/validation separation, original-coordinate validation ground truth, epoch histories, prediction coverage and geometry, training budget, and best/final checkpoint metadata were checked. [S4]
Independent COCO evaluation recomputed the selected epoch 18 and final epoch 20, including per-class metrics, with maximum discrepancy zero. All ten controller updates and all 15 exact-set states per update were reconstructed from saved per-object observations using an independent aggregation implementation. Training decisions, sample order, group geometry, fallbacks, and exposure counts were replayed across all 20 epochs. Earlier conditions retain their prior independent metric audits; the comparison also checked their original archive hashes and saved records.
These audits do not constitute independent reruns of the detector or raw-pixel pipeline. Full checkpoint tensors were not re-executed locally, and raw probe boxes/logits were not archived. Reported pixel-change counters and state-preservation flags were checked for consistency with source and records, not reproduced from a fresh live GPU model. Unit tests and successful training are complementary evidence, not substitutes for performance confirmation.
The principal scientific limitations are one seed per condition, checkpoint selection on the same validation set used for reporting, a narrow operation bank, no full YOLO or scratch-initialization replication, no matched fixed-strength realization control, unequal online forward cost, and unequal edit exposure after class-set fallbacks. Warmup trajectory differences limit causal attribution. The observed support and ROI issues identify plausible intervention targets, but do not prove which mechanism caused the AP result.
No test-set, unseen-country, deployment or repeated-seed result is available. Crop, training-subset and detection validation results answer different questions.
10 Review of the redesign and reasons for changing direction
Source: RDD_Methodology_Redesign_and_Execution_Plan.docx in the user’s Downloads folder. The document is a proposal to assess, not an instruction that automatically overrides experimental evidence. It was read and compared with the completed audits. Its two routes were Approach A, context-decoupled local synthesis, and Approach B, feature-space augmentation with an IoU curriculum. Neither route has been executed in this project.
10 1 Which criticisms are supported

Three corrections to older summaries: nine of fifteen class sets never activated (six did); 94.26% counts augmentation applications with near-uniform family probabilities, not policy states; and the dataset contains seven collections from six countries.
10 2 What Approach A proposed and what survived review
The external document proposed smooth distance-based masks, Fourier-amplitude perturbations, copy-paste with Poisson or color-space matching, factorization into four class-level policies, and an EMA-based GT-ROI observer. Its useful direction was to address both support fragmentation and the limited set of appearance-only interventions. Its technical guarantees and budget estimates were too strong.

The revision therefore retains smooth local rendering, class-pooled evidence and controlled context relocation as hypotheses to test. It removes the claims of zero-cost feedback, guaranteed semantic preservation, automatically correct mixtures and predetermined improvement.
10 3 Why Approach B is not the next experiment
Approach B would add ROI feature-style mixing, DropBlock and a rising IoU threshold, approximately 0.5 to 0.65. These changes move the intervention inside the detector and change supervision or feature distributions at the same time. They would make it harder to identify whether local augmentation policy is responsible for any change. Higher IoU thresholds can also reduce positive assignments when proposal quality is insufficient. The latest direction keeps this route deferred; it is not an implemented fallback or a second active experiment.
Relevant prior-art anchors discussed during review include Copy-Paste (arXiv:2012.07177), Fourier Domain Adaptation (arXiv:2004.05498), MixStyle (arXiv:2104.02008) and Cascade R-CNN (arXiv:1712.00726). Their established ingredients are not new contributions of this project. A new claim must concern a demonstrated residual capability of the complete, controlled method, not a renamed combination of known operations.
11 Latest version 2 methodology and unresolved specification
Status: conversational design only. The working description is class-adaptive group augmentation with whole-image geometry and controlled group copy-paste. The latest discussion supersedes the earlier suggestion to keep exact-set policies and merely switch to the weakest admissible strength. It does not supersede the recorded version-1 results. No version-2 experiment can be reported until a new protocol and implementation exist.
11 1 Research question and invariant training setup
The revised question is whether class-conditioned admissibility improves training when the action bank includes context and geometric variation, while avoiding independent edits to overlapping damage labels. The primary comparison is class-adaptive versus shared online selection under the same expanded bank. The proposed detector remains Faster R-CNN with ResNet-50 FPN and COCO initialization, using the existing frozen data splits, 640-pixel letterbox and 20-epoch training recipe. Architecture novelty is not claimed. A retention gate is a measured detection-stability rule, not proof of unchanged human semantics.
11 2 Three augmentation components

Horizontal flipping does not convert a transverse crack into a longitudinal crack by itself. A left-right reflection maps a horizontal line to a horizontal line and a vertical line to a vertical line; applied to the whole scene, it also reflects the road direction. The intended crack category is relative to the road, not merely to the x or y axis of the image. A 90-degree rotation of an isolated crack patch can change that relation and is excluded. Vertical flips, arbitrary local rotations and anisotropic stretching are also excluded from the initial bank. Even label-preserving whole-scene geometry still needs visual and annotation checks for realistic use.
For a continuous XYXY box in an image of width W, horizontal reflection maps [xmin, ymin, xmax, ymax] to [W − xmax, ymin, W − xmin, ymax]. Scale and translation must use the same affine transform for pixels and boxes. The implementation must follow the project’s continuous-coordinate convention rather than mix it with inclusive integer-pixel formulas.
11 3 Controlled group copy-paste
The proposed donor bank uses training images only. Sample a target class uniformly, then an eligible donor group containing that class; the exact handling of groups eligible for multiple classes still needs specification. Proposed uniform donor scales are 0.9, 1.0 and 1.1. Keep the group’s orientation and use compatible road/camera views rather than freely exchanging vehicle-camera and drone patches.
The patch must include the complete selected group, a defined context margin, and all annotations intersecting the copied region, or the candidate must be rejected. Its opaque interior must contain all copied target boxes. Feathering occurs outside that interior so that the blend does not erase the labeled damage. The source image is not modified. The recipient gains transformed labels for the copied objects. Non-target annotated objects also require explicit handling; they cannot silently become unlabeled artifacts.
Recipients are proposed to come from training images with no target boxes, using reviewed pasteable road regions. Reject placements that overlap known annotations or violate road/camera compatibility. An empty target list is not proof that the area contains no damage. The proposed copy-paste probability is 0.25 on eligible opportunities, leaving most negative training presentations unchanged. The donor bank, recipient bank, nominal opportunity schedule and rejection bookkeeping must be identical across the augmented comparators.
These banks and masks do not yet exist as a reviewed version-2 artifact. Building them is substantive dataset work, not a minor coding detail. If sufficiently reliable recipient regions cannot be obtained within the available time, copy-paste must be explicitly deferred and the scientific claim narrowed; it must not be replaced silently by arbitrary rectangle pasting.
11 4 Per-class admissibility and group decisions
Replace fifteen exact class-set policy tables with evidence pooled per damage class and candidate action. For each tested operation/strength, measure how many reference-detected objects remain correctly detected in the edited view. The proposed reference detection rule remains confidence at least 0.25 and IoU at least 0.5, with a retention threshold of 0.8. The proposed minimum support increases to sixteen reference-detected objects from eight distinct source clusters per class and action. These are engineering choices awaiting implementation and sensitivity assessment, not validated statistical guarantees.
A group containing several classes can use an action only when it is admissible for every constituent class. Sufficiently supported direct observations on that mixed group can veto an action that the pooled marginal estimates would admit. When a decision borrows per-class evidence because the exact mixture has little support, record that fact. When even a constituent class/action lacks support, retain identity. The exact mixed-group veto threshold has not yet been frozen.
The latest proposal does not maximize the clipped loss-increase score and does not automatically choose the strongest passing level. It uses balanced random sampling over eligible families, operations and tested strengths, with the precise hierarchy to be fixed in the configuration. The auxiliary loss remains a diagnostic. Thus version 2 is an adaptive admissibility controller, not a claim to directly optimize downstream learning gain.
This remains distinct from online shared local augmentation: the shared comparator uses one common admissibility policy across classes, whereas the adaptive controller routes a group through the intersection of its constituent class policies. If the resulting class policies are identical in practice, the two methods may behave similarly; logs must expose that outcome rather than assume differentiation.
11 5 Probe observer and update loop
The proposed update schedule remains before epochs 2, 4, 6, …, 20, after one common unaugmented warmup epoch. Probe the frozen detector on training-only panels without optimizer, gradient, parameter, normalization-buffer or global-RNG changes. Ordinary detector predictions provide the retention measurement. Use matched GT boxes and deterministic nearby ROIs, fixed or geometrically mapped across views, for a comparable auxiliary loss diagnostic; GT ROIs must never be inserted into inference predictions.
For an in-place edit, compare the same objects before and after the edit. For copy-paste, compare donor objects with their transformed copies and inspect recipient detections before and after pasting, including false positives outside the pasted region. The mapping of boxes, retained support, source clusters and recipient context must be archived. Feedback is not zero cost: record backbone image forwards, auxiliary ROI work, wall time and failed/restarted probes. The exact panel allocation and forward budget are still unresolved and must be matched between the two online arms.
At each update: select the frozen probe panel, render candidate views, collect detection and diagnostic observations, validate support, build the admissible-action sets, commit the controller together with model/optimizer/RNG state, then resume ordinary detector training. During inference and validation, use the detector alone without augmentation or ground-truth-dependent controller routing.
11 6 New comparison arms and experimental sequence

Create a common unaugmented warmup checkpoint for each seed, then branch the four arms from the same model, optimizer, scheduler and random states. Use the same subsequent image order and separate keyed augmentation streams. Check actual repeatability on the target CUDA stack; warn_only=True permits nondeterministic operations and is not a determinism guarantee. Record any remaining limits.
Execution order: one consolidated renderer/annotation/controller preflight; a matched short pilot; then the full 20-epoch comparison only if the pipeline has usable support, correct annotations, reproducible checkpoint recovery and acceptable measured cost. After the protocol is frozen, use matched repeated seeds and the necessary component ablations. Reserve the test partition for a final settled comparison. Do not repeatedly tune against it.
Primary endpoint: selected validation AP50:95 under the frozen selection rule. Secondary diagnostics include AP50, AP75, per-class AP, negative-image false alarms, localization errors, final-epoch metrics, applied/changed/rejected/fallback counts, policy differences across classes and total compute. A copy-paste-removal ablation is needed for a claim about the context intervention. A renderer ablation is needed if smoother masks are claimed to cause a gain. The old version-1 runs remain historical evidence; they cannot serve as matched controls for a changed operation bank.

Issue | Evidence and verdict
Exact class-set sparsity | Supported. Nine of fifteen possible sets never obtained an active policy. Mixed-class opportunities fell back to identity 58.52% of the time. Training examples do exist for several affected sets; the probe support was the limitation.
Strength-selection rule | Supported as a design limitation. The strongest passing level was selected even when it had zero clipped difficulty or a lower level had a positive score. Stronger appearance change is not automatically more useful training.
Auxiliary loss comparability | Supported measurement concern. Reference and edited views used different positive proposals. ROI counts changed in 86.64% of the examined pairs, so loss changes cannot be interpreted purely as the effect on the same object samples.
Narrow action bank | Fact. Version 1 used six photometric operations plus blur and noise. It did not test context relocation, copy-paste or a broad geometric bank. The missed benefit of those alternatives is still a hypothesis.
Mask boundaries caused failure | Unproven. A narrow taper could introduce artifacts, but no renderer ablation isolated its effect on accuracy. Smooth blending is a proposed intervention, not an established cure.
Class imbalance caused failure | Unproven causal claim. Class frequencies differ, but the five runs did not isolate imbalance from other mechanisms. Sampling changes need their own controls.
CUDA nondeterminism explains scores | Not established. Pre-augmentation epoch-1 trajectories differed, but neither repeated-run variance nor the precise source of those differences was measured.
Original assertion or suggestion | Assessment carried into the revision
Preserving Fourier phase preserves crack semantics | Not a guarantee. Amplitude changes can alter fine-edge visibility and contrast; blur is a simple counterexample to phase alone being sufficient. Fourier augmentation was not retained in the initial version-2 bank.
GT ROIs and no_grad make feedback zero cost | False. Clean and transformed features still require backbone work unless explicitly reused in a valid way. Version 2 must record additional image forwards, ROI work and wall time.
Four class estimates solve mixed-set behavior | Pooling improves sample availability, but marginal evidence does not prove safety for every overlap combination. Version 2 adds joint admissibility and a supported mixed-group veto.
Use a simple minimum of maximum safe levels | Unsafe without monotonicity. Each tested action/strength must retain its own admissibility result; a failing lower level cannot be assumed safe because a higher one passed.
Copy individual defects from RGB boxes | The data has boxes, not verified instance masks or crack skeletons. An RGB box contains pavement/context. Version 2 proposes copying an annotated group with context and explicit label closure, not pretending it is a segmented object.
Pasting into no-target images gives clean background | No-target annotations do not establish healthy pavement or a valid road region. Recipient areas need review; background and camera compatibility cannot be assumed.
Fixed speedup and success thresholds | The proposed 3–5× minority exposure, 95 GPU-hour plan, arbitrary strength caps and early-epoch AP gates were not demonstrated or accepted as evidence. Measure cost and freeze justified gates before running.
Component | Proposed behavior and annotation rule
Shared whole-image geometry | Horizontal reflection, modest isotropic scaling and translation on a fixed canvas. Transform every box with the entire image. Reject transformations that crop annotated damage under the declared policy. Use the same geometry distribution in every new arm.
In-place local appearance | Retain the current appearance families as candidates, render one connected overlap group once, and replace the very narrow taper with a specified smooth blend. Original target boxes remain valid because objects do not move.
Controlled group copy-paste | Copy a complete annotated overlap group with surrounding context to a reviewed compatible road region. Transfer every included annotation, maintain orientation, and preserve the original donor. This is a context-patch intervention with boxes, not mask-based instance transplantation.
New arm | Role
Standard geometry only | Controls for the shared whole-image geometric bank. It is a new baseline, not the historical identity run.
Fixed expanded augmentation | Uses the same local appearance and copy-paste renderer/banks with a fixed declared sampling distribution.
Online shared expanded augmentation | Uses feedback to define one common admissible-action distribution. Controls for online selection without class routing.
Online class-adaptive expanded augmentation | Uses per-class pooled evidence and joint group admissibility. Primary comparator: the online shared expanded arm.
Unresolved item | Minimum decision needed
Whole-image geometry | Flip probability, scale and translation bounds, canvas padding, interpolation, clipping rules and precise rejection of cropped annotations.
Appearance and blending | Version-2 candidate magnitudes, application probability, context margin, mask function/width and handling of adjacent annotations. Do not assume every version-1 number was already accepted for version 2.
Donor and recipient banks | Review procedure, saved region format, source/camera compatibility, annotation closure, donor reuse limits and bank size feasible within the project deadline.
Sampling and exposure | How multi-class donor groups are weighted, the probability hierarchy, rejection caps/fallbacks and how nominal opportunities and realized exposure are compared.
Observer | Deterministic nearby-ROI geometry and assignments, loss aggregation, transformed-ROI clipping and the exact mixed-group veto/support rule.
Probe and compute budget | Panel composition, number of candidate views, repeated noise draws, matched online forward count and a measured pilot timing budget.
Experimental decision rule | Pilot length, predeclared continuation criteria, repeated-seed count, uncertainty analysis and ablations justified by the claims. Three seeds alone do not guarantee significance.
Course scope | Whether using a standardized COCO-pretrained detector satisfies the original course requirement for a custom CNN trained from scratch. Instructor approval is not established in the records.
